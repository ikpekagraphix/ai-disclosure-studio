AI labeling FAQ

General information, last reviewed November 2026. Rules change, so check official sources or a qualified lawyer.

What does EU AI Act Article 50 require?

Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026. Providers must tell people when they are interacting with AI (unless obvious) and mark generated output in a machine-readable way. Some systems already on the market before that date have until 2 December 2026 to meet the marking duty. Deployers must disclose deepfakes. They must also disclose AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, unless it had human review or editorial control and someone holds editorial responsibility. Other duties cover emotion recognition and biometric categorisation. Exceptions apply, including lighter disclosure for evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional deepfakes.

Is a visible AI badge enough to comply with the EU AI Act?

Not necessarily. A visible label can help a deployer disclose content, which should be clear and available no later than first exposure. It is not the machine-readable marking that Article 50(2) requires from AI system providers. This tool does not add invisible watermarks or C2PA credentials. What you must do depends on your role, the content and the context.

Do I need to label AI content on YouTube, TikTok and LinkedIn?

YouTube: disclose realistic altered or synthetic content using the setting in YouTube Studio. Using AI only for ideas or scripts generally does not trigger it. TikTok: turn on its AI-generated content setting for realistic AI-generated or significantly edited media. LinkedIn: clearly disclose synthetic or manipulated media that shows real people in ways that could mislead. A caption from this tool does not replace a platform's own setting.

Is there a US AI disclosure law?

There is no single federal AI labeling law. Rules vary by state and use. New York has required disclosure of synthetic performers in certain ads since June 2026. California's AI Transparency Act places duties mainly on covered technology providers and platforms, and California has separate synthetic performer ad rules scheduled for 2027. General rules against deceptive advertising still apply.
